# Base model Extended Data figure legends

**Extended Data Figure 1.** Connection composition and household transitions in the base realization. a, Household shares in formal, direct informal service-drop, informal hook-on and unconnected states over 1,480 model weeks. b, The first 52 weeks without smoothing. c, Row-normalized transition frequencies between adjacent end-of-week formal, informal and unconnected states, pooled over weeks 1–1,480. The informal category combines service drops and hook-ons. Rows sum to 100%; diagonal cells denote unchanged observed status. Transitions are reconstructed from household states and do not use the model's mixed switch counter. Changes reversed within a week and changes between informal subtypes are not resolved. One realization, seed 42; 2,000 households and 100 poles. No uncertainty intervals are implied.

**Extended Data Figure 2.** Boolean provider attitudes and their association with connection status. a, Percentage of households with a positive KPLC or informal power distributor (IPD) attitude at every recorded week. b, The four joint attitude states, which partition the population. c,d, Positive attitudes conditional on end-of-week connection status at weeks 52 and 1,480; symbols show the complete observed group and n is the household count. Both attitudes are Boolean flags, not continuous trust scores. The selected blackout rule can reduce KPLC attitudes among formal and informal customers. These panels describe associations and do not isolate a causal effect of attitudes on provider choice. One seed-42 realization; no smoothing or confidence intervals.

**Extended Data Figure 3.** Outage exposure and the pole-usage accumulator in the current implementation. a, Fractions of poles marked overloaded and households exposed to overloaded supply. Household exposure is captured immediately after the blackout check, before consumption, switching or enforcement, and is plotted at the completed week's tick. b, Outage flags for all 100 poles during weeks 1–104, ordered by pole identifier. c, End-of-week accumulated usage divided by capacity for an objectively selected pole during weeks 0–52; shading denotes outage weeks and the dashed line denotes capacity. The selected pole has an outage fraction closest to the median among poles that ever overload; ties use identifier order. Usage accumulates across weeks and is reset during outage handling. This is an implementation diagnostic, not an instantaneous power-flow calculation. One seed-42 realization; no smoothing. Initial exposure is zero before any go call.

**Extended Data Figure 4.** Price assumptions, connection installments and income differences in access and spending. a, Median predicted monthly cost divided by desired monthly electricity spending within initial income deciles (200 households each). Formal scenarios assume full demand, either with a 625-unit installment or after repayment; informal cost is the initial flat fee. The dotted line denotes desired spending. b, Formal access by fixed initial income quintile (400 households each), at setup and weeks 52 and 1,480. c, Fraction of currently formal households with outstanding installments. d, Month-end electricity expenditure as a fraction of income, across all households within each quintile. Symbols show medians; whiskers show interquartile ranges, not confidence intervals. The dashed line gives median desired spending. Weeks 52 and 1,480 both close a four-week billing month. Recorded expenses can include multiple connection arrangements within that month. These are descriptive differences within one seed-42 realization and do not identify causal effects of repayment or income.
